# DineHop features

DineHop combines restaurant discovery, comparison, saving and outing planning in one app. This page describes the major features currently available in the Android app. The iOS app is under development.

This page was last reviewed on 16 August 2026.

## Feature summary

| Area | What it includes | Account needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search and discovery | Search by dish, cuisine, venue, neighbourhood, postcode or area | No |
| Results and map | List and map views, filters, sorting and area search | No |
| Place details | Menus, hours, photos, prices, ratings, reviews and contact links | No |
| Food hygiene | Official ratings or inspection information where supported | No |
| Shortlist and Hops | Collect candidates and organise a named multi-stop outing | No to start; an account enables syncing |
| Saved places | Private notes, search, filters, sorting and area groups | An account enables syncing across sessions |
| Community reviews | Read reviews or publish a rating and written review | No to read; a verified account is needed to publish |
| Profile and account | Identity, contributions, privacy controls and account management | Yes |

## Search by what you want to eat

DineHop supports searches based on the way people commonly describe a meal or destination:

- A dish, such as ramen or pizza.
- A cuisine, such as Japanese or Lebanese.
- A restaurant or venue name.
- A neighbourhood, town, city, postcode or broader area.
- The current device location, when location access is allowed.

Search suggestions help resolve places and queries. Recent searches make it easier to repeat a search without entering it again.

The home screen also provides category browsing for common needs such as restaurants, cafes, breakfast, desserts, pizza, fast food and places suitable for children. Search and category availability can vary by location.

## Compare results in a list or on a map

Search results are available as both a list and an interactive map. The two views share the active search and filter state, so moving between them does not create a separate set of results.

Result cards can show available information such as:

- Restaurant name, category and cuisine.
- Rating and review count.
- Price level or an estimated budget range.
- Current open or closed status.
- Distance from the selected location.
- A representative photo.
- Saved, shortlisted and visited state.

Results can be sorted by relevance, distance or rating. Available filters include:

- Open now.
- Cuisine.
- Halal, vegan, vegetarian and gluten-free needs.
- Price band or a maximum budget in local currency.
- Minimum rating, with a review-count threshold.
- Places already seen or visited.
- Takeaway and fast-food inclusion where relevant.

On the map, users can move to a different area and run **Search this area**. Selecting a map marker opens the corresponding place card for comparison.

Individual places can be hidden when they are not useful. This removes clutter from the current decision without changing the venue's source data.

## Open a detailed place profile

A place profile brings together the available information needed to evaluate a restaurant before leaving DineHop. Depending on the venue and its data sources, it can include:

- A photo gallery with full-screen image viewing.
- Address, map position and distance.
- Live direction and compass guidance from the current location.
- Opening hours and current open, closed or closing-soon status.
- Price information, rating and review count.
- Cuisine, venue type and an editorial summary where available.
- Menu content or a menu link when a confirmed source is available.
- Google reviews and DineHop community reviews.
- Official food-hygiene information in supported areas.

Available actions can include calling the venue, opening its website, viewing a menu, sharing the place and starting directions in a mapping app.

Restaurant details come from third-party and official sources. A field may be unavailable when the source does not publish it, and published information can change over time.

## Check official food-hygiene information

Where DineHop can match a restaurant to a supported public record, the place profile shows information from the relevant official food-hygiene or inspection authority. DineHop shows the source and update date with the record and does not create its own inspection score.

Current support covers official schemes in eight countries:

- Country-wide coverage where records are available in the United Kingdom, France, Singapore, Finland, Denmark and Norway.
- City-level coverage in New York City, Chicago and Austin in the United States.
- City-level coverage in Toronto, Canada.

A missing rating does not mean that a venue failed an inspection. It can mean that no supported record was available or that DineHop could not confidently match the venue. See [food-hygiene and inspection coverage](product-facts.md#food-hygiene-and-inspection-coverage) for the named schemes and authorities.

## Build a Shortlist

The Shortlist is a temporary collection for places currently under consideration. A venue can be added from search results, the map or its place profile.

This is useful when comparing several options before deciding where to go. Shortlisted places can later be added to a Hop, removed from consideration or opened again for more detail.

## Plan a multi-stop outing with Hops

A **Hop** is a named plan containing several places. It can be used for a progressive dinner, a cafe route, an evening across several venues or any outing where one saved destination is not enough.

A Hop can include:

- A custom name.
- Multiple restaurants or other food venues.
- A chosen order for the stops.
- An optional planned date.
- A private planning note.
- A visited state for each stop.
- An optional would-return verdict after visiting.

Users can create and edit Hops while signed out. Signing in enables account-based syncing and helps keep personal planning data associated with the account.

## Keep a searchable Saved collection

Saving is intended for places worth keeping beyond the current search. A saved place can include a private note, making the collection useful for personal context such as a dish to try, who recommended it or why the venue was saved.

Saved-place search matches information including:

- Restaurant name.
- Cuisine.
- Address.
- Private note text.

The Saved collection can be filtered by open status, cuisine, city or area, visit intention and would-return verdict. It can be sorted by nearest, recently saved or name. When browsing a larger collection, places can be grouped by city or area.

An account enables saved places and their personal metadata to be associated with the user and synced.

## Record where you went

Places can be marked as visited. A would-return verdict can then preserve a simple personal decision for later searches and planning.

Visited state is also useful when filtering results or reviewing a Hop. It is separate from publishing a community review, so a private personal record does not have to become public content.

## Read and publish reviews

DineHop can show two distinct kinds of review content:

- Third-party reviews supplied by Google, which are presented as source-attributed reference material.
- DineHop community reviews written by DineHop users.

Community reviews can be read without an account. Publishing requires an account, and email-based accounts must verify their email address before contributing. A published review includes a rating and can include written comments.

Submissions are checked for prohibited language. Review reporting, moderation and the [DineHop Community Guidelines](https://dinehop.app/guidelines) provide a process for content that may violate the rules.

## Use local language and formats

DineHop supports ten interface languages:

- English
- German
- Spanish
- French
- Italian
- Portuguese
- Japanese
- Korean
- Indonesian
- Arabic, including right-to-left layout

Budget controls use local currency where supported, and distance presentation follows the relevant local format.

## Manage an account and profile

Basic search, map browsing and place details do not require an account. Accounts are used for synced personal information and public contributions.

Account and profile features include:

- Email or Google sign-in.
- Email verification and password reset.
- A display name and profile image.
- Public or private profile visibility.
- Access to the user's published reviews and contributions.
- In-app account deletion with identity confirmation.

Account deletion instructions are also available on the [DineHop website](https://dinehop.app/delete-account).

## Availability and service boundaries

- **Android:** Available on [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=restaurant.app).
- **iOS:** Under development. No public release date has been announced.
- **Price:** Free to use and supported by advertising. There is no subscription.
- **Restaurant discovery:** Available worldwide, subject to local third-party data coverage.
- **Bookings:** DineHop does not currently provide table reservations.
- **Ordering:** DineHop does not currently provide food ordering or delivery.
- **Public API:** No public application programming interface is currently offered.
- **Source code:** The application source code is not published in this repository.
